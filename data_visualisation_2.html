A Five-Year Glance Inside Australia's Changing Hospital Network

Hospital use has recovered and grown since the COVID-19 years. Explore where activity is rising, how care differs across ages and services, and what the national hospital network looks like.

SEPARATION
One completed episode of hospital care, from admission to discharge. One person can have several.
PATIENT DAY
One day (or part of a day) that a patient spends in hospital. Longer stays add more patient days.

01

DEMAND AND PLACE

Hospital activity is growing, but not evenly

Start with the whole system: how Australia's hospital capacity compares with other countries, how public and private activity has changed since 2020-21, and how public hospital use differs between states and territories.

01

Hospital beds per 1,000 people by country

Australia has 3.8 hospital beds for every 1,000 people, ranking 41 of 141 countries with data.

Japan, North Korea, and South Korea have the most, at more than 12.5 per 1,000 people (3.4 times Australia's level), while Chad has the fewest, at 0.1.

Countries without a reported value are grey. Where the source substitutes a nearby year for 2021, the tooltip shows that original data year.

02

Hospital separations by sector are rising

From 2020-21 to 2024-25, public hospital separations rose 10% (to 7.7 million) and private hospital separations rose 5% (to 5.1 million). Public hospitals accounted for 60% of separations in 2024-25.

03

Public hospital use per person differs across states and territories

Northern Territory has the highest rate, at 681 public separations per 1,000 people. That is 3.0 times the lowest rate, in Western Australia (229).

    PEOPLE AND CONDITIONS

    Age and diagnosis shape the need for care

    Those totals are made up of people with very different needs. The next three charts ask which conditions are treated most often and how age shapes the time people spend in hospital, where older Australians account for a large share.

    04

    Kidney and urinary conditions lead these diagnostic groups

    Kidney and Urinary Tract is the largest diagnostic group, with 2.2 million separations.

    The three largest groups (Kidney and Urinary Tract, Digestive System, and Musculoskeletal System and Connective Tissue) together make up 41% of the acute separations shown.

    05

    Total hospital separations vary across age groups

    Females account for 52% of the separations shown. Between ages 20 and 39, female separations are significantly higher than male separations, largely because of pregnancy and childbirth. From age 65 onwards, male separations are higher.

    06

    Australians aged 65+ account for over half of patient days

    People aged 65 and over account for 52% of patient days (17.9 of 34.3 million). The single largest age group is 75-79 years, with 3.9 million patient days. The 0-4 age group also has many patient days, largely from care around childbirth in hospital.

    03

    HOW CARE IS DELIVERED

    A hospital episode can mean a visit or a longer stay

    Time in hospital is only part of the story. These charts separate short visits from longer stays, then follow how public and private hospitals deliver different kinds of care.

    07

    Public hospitals account for most patient days

    From 2020-21 to 2024-25, public hospital patient days rose 15% and private hospital patient days rose 1%. Public hospitals accounted for 70% of patient days in 2024-25.

    08

    Same-day care makes up most separations

    Same-day stays made up 64% of separations (8.2 of 12.8 million), almost double the share of overnight stays (36%). This may reflect more patients having less serious episodes that do not need an overnight stay.

    09

    Hospital sectors provide a different mix of services

    Non-emergency care is the largest service type, accounting for 54% of separations in public hospitals and 82% in private hospitals. Most public hospital services are medical (70%), while private hospital services are more spread out. Public hospitals account for 77% of childbirths, while private hospitals account for 82% of rehabilitation.

    04

    WHERE HOSPITALS AND PATIENTS ARE

    Hospitals follow the population, yet remote residents use them most

    Finally, the story moves from what hospitals do to where they are. Hospital locations cluster where people live, but when each area's population is taken into account, residents of very remote areas have the highest rates of hospital use.

    10

    Hospitals cluster around Australia's population centres

    New South Wales has the most hospital locations (287 of 877). New South Wales, Queensland and Victoria together hold 75% of all mapped hospitals. Most hospitals are on the east coast, where most Australians live.

    11

    Hospital use is highest among people living in very remote areas

    In 2023-24, very remote areas had 817 separations per 1,000 people, double the rate in major cities (410). In every year shown, hospital use is higher in remote and very remote areas than in cities and regional areas. The biggest step is from remote to very remote areas, where rates differ by 185 to 303 separations per 1,000 people across the years.

    WHAT THE CHARTS ADD UP TO

    A growing system, used most by older Australians and by people far from cities

    Both public and private hospitals are providing more episodes of care than in 2020-21, and most episodes are short, same-day visits. Older Australians account for a large share of the time spent in hospital, hospitals cluster where people live, and residents of very remote areas have the highest rates of use. Taken together, hospital demand depends on who people are and where they live, not only on how many people there are.
